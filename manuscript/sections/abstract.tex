\begin{abstract}
Quantitative real-time PCR (qPCR) is a standard technique for measuring gene
expression, yet turning instrument-exported $C_q$ values into
publication-ready results requires a chain of non-trivial steps:
amplification-efficiency estimation, choice of quantification method,
statistical comparison of groups, and figure preparation. The established
tools for this chain are predominantly programming-based---including our own
R package \textit{qPCRtools}---and therefore remain out of reach for part of
the laboratory community, notably students and wet-lab researchers without
programming experience; instrument-vendor packages and free stand-alone or
web-based tools cover individual steps, but tie the analysis to one
instrument platform, to one step, or to uploading the data to a remote
server. Here we present \textit{qPCRtools}, a
cross-platform desktop application written in C++17 with Qt6 that exposes the
analytical core of the R package through a point-and-click interface and
ships as a single executable requiring no R, Python, or other runtime
environment. The application implements standard-curve construction and
amplification-efficiency estimation, the $\Delta C_t$, $\Delta\Delta C_t$ and
efficiency-based (RqPCR) relative quantification methods, group comparison by
two-sample $t$-test, Wilcoxon rank-sum test, or one-way ANOVA with Tukey's HSD
letter grouping, and one-click export of the summary table, the per-replicate
raw data and a plain-text analysis record of the inputs and parameters used.
We validated the C++ implementation against
the R package on identical inputs: across every shared quantification method
the compared quantities agree to floating-point precision, and the validation
is reproducible from the open scripts shipped with the software. Preparing
the port also revealed and corrected several defects in result aggregation,
each covered by a regression test.
\textit{qPCRtools} is freely available under the MIT license for Windows and
macOS, and its source code compiles on Linux.
\end{abstract}
