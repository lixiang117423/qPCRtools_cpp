\subsection{Typical analysis workflow}

{\sloppy We applied the application to the bundled example dataset
(\texttt{examples/cq.csv}, \texttt{examples/design.csv}): the target gene
\textit{fos-glo-myc} against the reference gene \textit{Beta Actin} across
three treatment groups (0, 0.5, 1) with three biological replicates each.
With the $\Delta\Delta C_t$ method and Welch tests against the control, the
mean relative expression was 1.01, 1.92 and 2.74 in the three groups
($n = 3$ biological replicates). The comparisons against the control had
unadjusted $P = 0.0060$ and $P = 0.0222$; after Holm adjustment
they were $P = 0.0121$ and $P = 0.0222$. As defined by Livak and Schmittgen,
the calibrator is the control group. These values are reproduced without the
application by \texttt{validation/}\allowbreak
\texttt{check\_example\_stats.R}, and the complete
output is shown in Supplementary Figure~S1B. On a four-fold dilution series
of four genes, the standard-curve module recovered slopes of $-2.07$ to
$-2.24$ $C_q$ per dilution step. Three curves had $R^2 > 0.99$ and
efficiencies of 0.85--0.94; Gene1 had $R^2 = 0.75$ and was correctly flagged
as unsuitable. The default 90--110\% rule additionally flagged Gene3 (85.5\%)
and Gene4 (87.3\%), and the quality-control panel flagged the single
$C_q = 40$ well of Gene1.

In a second analysis of the two published example plates distributed with the
2022 \textit{qPCRtools} paper, we used the dilution series to fit four curves
and the separate treatment plate to compare five target genes with
\textit{OsUBQ} in two groups with four biological replicates. Gene1 failed
quality control ($R^2 = 0.7509$), the other curves had
$R^2 = 0.9983$--$0.9993$, and the supplied gene-specific efficiencies
selected standard-curve expression rather than $2^{-\Delta\Delta C_t}$.
Welch tests with Holm adjustment gave $P = 0.0368$ for \textit{OsPR10} and
$P = 0.00230$ for \textit{OsWAK91}; the other three genes were not
significant. The workflow exported summary and replicate-level tables, both
figures and a parameter record, and is reproduced by a deterministic checker
(Supplementary Table~S3). The two plates are separate published examples and
are not presented as one biological experiment; the complete
workflow---import, method selection, analysis and export---was performed in
the graphical interface without writing code.\par}

\subsection{Validation against an independent reference}

{\sloppy We compared the application with an independent base-R implementation
of the three quantification methods---$\Delta\Delta C_t$, the standard curve
and the efficiency-based RqPCR method---using the same on-disk inputs
(Supplementary Table~S1 and Figure~S2). The reference was computed directly
from the raw $C_q$ tables and did not call the R package.
Across 58 matched quantities, expression values, standard deviations,
standard errors, raw and Holm-adjusted $P$ values, slopes, intercepts,
$R^2$ values and efficiencies agreed to floating-point precision (maximum
relative difference $1.9 \times 10^{-14}$). Every point falls on the line
$y = x$.\par}

The validation is reproducible from the open scripts in \texttt{validation/}
(see Availability). Numerical agreement establishes consistency with the
specified algorithms, not biological correctness, and the choice of scale
remains a design decision. Preparing the port also revealed defects in result
aggregation, including the input of the reference-gene stability measure,
replicate handling in the efficiency method and the $\Delta\Delta C_t$
calibrator, as well as multiple-comparison $P$ values in the C++ code. Each
was corrected and is covered by a regression test. Earlier GUI releases
contained these defects and should be re-run with version~1.3.1. The
corresponding R package is available as version~2.0.1, and version~1.0.2 is
available from CRAN. A retrospective assessment of the 2022 paper's example
analyses is provided with the R-package release.
