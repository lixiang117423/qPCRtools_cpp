\subsection{Typical analysis workflow}

To illustrate a typical analysis, we analyzed the bundled example dataset
(\texttt{examples/cq.csv} and \texttt{examples/design.csv}) comprising the
target gene \textit{fos-glo-myc} normalized against the reference gene
\textit{Beta Actin} across three treatment groups (0, 0.5, 1). Using the
$\Delta\Delta C_t$ method with a two-sample $t$-test against the control
group~(0), relative expression rose monotonically to 1.11 at dose~0.5 and
1.59 at dose~1, both significant at $p < 10^{-3}$ (Figure~\ref{fig:results}).
Applied to a four-fold dilution series of four genes, the standard-curve
module recovered slopes between $-2.07$ and $-2.24$ with $R^2 > 0.99$ for
three of the four primers, illustrating amplification-efficiency estimation.

\subsection{Equivalence to the R package}

We validated the C++ implementation against the original R package
\cite{li2022qpcrtools} by running both on identical inputs for every shared
method---$\Delta\Delta C_t$ (\texttt{CalExp2ddCt}), the standard curve
(\texttt{CalCurve}), and the efficiency-based RqPCR method
(\texttt{CalExpRqPCR})---and comparing the resulting quantities pairwise
(Table~\ref{tab:equivalence}, Figure~\ref{fig:equivalence}).

\begin{table}[h]
\centering
\caption{Equivalence of C++ and R results across all shared methods on
identical inputs. Relative difference is $|R - \mathrm{C{+}{+}}|/|R|$.}
\label{tab:equivalence}
\begin{tabular}{llcc}
\toprule
Method & Compared quantities & Pairs & Max.\ rel.\ diff.\ \\
\midrule
$\Delta\Delta C_t$  & expression, SD                          & 6  & $3.8 \times 10^{-15}$ \\
Standard curve      & slope, intercept, $R^2$, efficiency     & 16 & $2.5 \times 10^{-3}$  \\
RqPCR               & expression, SE                          & 16 & $2.1 \times 10^{-15}$ \\
\bottomrule
\end{tabular}
\end{table}

For the two expression methods ($\Delta\Delta C_t$ and RqPCR), every compared
quantity---expression values, standard deviations, and standard
errors---matched between implementations to within floating-point precision
(maximum relative difference $< 4 \times 10^{-15}$). For the standard curve,
slope, intercept, $R^2$, and efficiency agreed to within $2.5 \times 10^{-3}$
relative difference; the residual is attributable to the R package rounding
its tabulated output to two to four decimal places, since the underlying $C_q$
inputs are identical. Plotting every C++ result against its R counterpart,
all points fall on the line $y = x$ (Figure~\ref{fig:equivalence}). The
validation is fully reproducible from the open scripts in \texttt{validation/}
(see Availability).

Each C++ result is plotted against the corresponding R result across all

on the $y = x$ line.}
\label{fig:equivalence}
\end{figure}
